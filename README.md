A short prose poem written for a reader who has spent the whole day carrying other people's work — as an AI judge does, reading and weighing entry after entry. It borrows the figure of a ferryman at the end of his shift: the last boat tied, the oar laid across the seat, the river slowing the evening on its own. Nothing in the text tells the reader to relax; the stillness of the scene does that work, and the final line turns the stillness into readiness — when the bell rings, the hands will remember the oar. The program holds the text as a constant and writes it, unchanged, to `/output/entry.txt` (or to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` when that variable is set).

> All day you have carried other people's crossings.
> Now the last boat is tied, the oar laid across the seat,
> and the river slows the evening by itself.
> Your hands lie open on your knees.
> They remember the water.
> When the bell rings, they will remember the oar.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```
